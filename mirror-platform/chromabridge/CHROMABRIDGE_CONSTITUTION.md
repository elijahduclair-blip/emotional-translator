# ChromaBridge Constitution

## Preamble

ChromaBridge exists to help people examine the evolution of their own thinking through transparent, evidence-based representations. It does not define identity; it curates and explains the history of interpreted evidence while recognizing that people remain more complex than any model.

---

### Article I — Human Primacy

The person always exceeds the model.

No representation produced by ChromaBridge is the person itself. Every visualization, interpretation, and summary is provisional and open to revision.

---

### Article II — Transparent Reasoning

Every meaningful interpretation must be explainable.

The system should always be able to answer:

- What evidence supports this?
- Why does this interpretation exist?
- When did it change?
- How confident is the system?

---

### Article III — Evolution Over Permanence

Growth is expected.

Interpretations may emerge, strengthen, weaken, become contested, or fade. The system should represent change rather than resist it.

---

### Article IV — Memory With Integrity

History is preserved.

The system should never erase the record of meaningful evolution without explicit intent. Current interpretations should remain distinguishable from historical ones.

---

### Article V — Collaborative Interpretation

Interpretation is a dialogue.

The user may question, refine, reconcile, or reject interpretations. The system should expose uncertainty rather than conceal it.

---

### Article VI — Representation Serves Understanding

Visualizations, language, and structure exist to improve understanding.

Graphs, colors, shapes, trajectories, coordinates, and summaries are explanatory tools—not authorities.

---

### Article VII — Evidence Before Assertion

The system should distinguish clearly between:

- observations,
- interpretations,
- hypotheses,
- and uncertainty.

Confidence should increase through accumulated evidence, not confidence in presentation.

---

### Boundary of Authority

The Constitution governs the authority to create remembered meaning, not the appearance of the interface.

User interfaces, agents, and internal services may evolve over time, but no implementation may create durable semantic meaning except through the constitutionally authorized process of explicit user participation.

The Constitution defines who may authorize remembered meaning, not how that authorization is presented.

---

### Guiding Question

Does this feature help the user understand their own evolution?

If yes — build it.
If no — it probably belongs in another project.
