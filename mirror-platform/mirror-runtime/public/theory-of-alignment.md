# Theory of Alignment

The Theory of Alignment treats meaning as relational rather than as an isolated fixed label.

Its smallest working loop is:

1. Identify an observation.
2. Compare it with another observation.
3. Record similarities and differences.
4. Repeat across time and context.
5. Keep only patterns that survive review and counterexamples.

The graph is memory. Color is a translation and tracing layer. Context comes from the ordered conversation. A failed comparison is useful evidence and must be allowed to weaken a proposed relationship.
