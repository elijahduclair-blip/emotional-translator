About the Garden

Information enters.
Useful fruit returns.

Community Garden is a protected environment where a person can bring a question, observation, or unfinished thought. The system preserves what was said, translates its structure, examines governed relationships, and returns an understandable response.

The Garden metaphor

Knowledge develops over time. Rooms organize different kinds of work. A local room can focus on one task, connect to bounded services, and save approved results without exposing the Garden's internal roots.

A continuing loop

  1. Receive information.
  2. Translate and account for its structure.
  3. Consult relevant relationships and context.
  4. Compose useful fruit.
  5. Review, revisit, and grow.

Shared knowledge

Approved graph knowledge supplies stable relationships and source-backed references. New conversation does not silently rewrite that shared map.

Personal cultivation

A signed-in person's reviewed context and ordered conversation can guide translation inside that person's private plot. It does not become another person's context.

Three cooperating systems

Qwen supplies candidate words and outside information. ARI performs the accountable relational translation. ChromaBridge protects meaning boundaries and proposal-only governance. Their responsibilities remain separate even while the experience feels like one Garden.

Community Garden can adapt how information is presented. It cannot turn a successful response into unchecked authority or silently grant itself permission to rewrite its foundations.
