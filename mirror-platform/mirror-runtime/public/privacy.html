Privacy and boundaries

Protected roots.
Accountable growth.

Community Garden separates what is public, what belongs to one person's private plot, what may become reviewed learning, and what must remain below the soil.

Public cultivation

A visitor may submit a bounded seed and receive a response without an account. Guest seeds are not stored as personal memory and cannot inspect private plots or administrative tools.

Personal plots

A signed-in person may use their own reviewed relationships and ordered conversation for context. One account cannot read or modify another account's transcript, traits, or relationship overlay.

Conversation memory

Person statements and AI responses can remain in their original order as a private transcript. This supports continuity and auditability; it does not automatically become community knowledge or training data.

Reviewed learning

Approval, correction, or explanation may create a proposed cultivation record. A successful response alone does not authorize graph mutation, code changes, or shared learning.

Operational analytics

Garden analytics track content-free operational measures such as room entry, response timing, status, and reviewed decisions. Outside-weather analytics use aggregated edge metadata rather than message content or raw personal transcripts.

Protected services

Service credentials, bearer tokens, internal model traces, database records, and graph controls do not cross the public entrance into browser JavaScript.

Meaning boundaries

Color-climate orientation and Braille structure help ARI trace and translate information. They do not diagnose a person, change the facts, grant semantic authority, or rewrite the Color Atlas.

Privacy is part of the architecture: public, personal, shared, and administrative soil remain distinct even when the Garden presents one connected experience.
